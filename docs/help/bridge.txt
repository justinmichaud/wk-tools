putting a segment that cannot join the tailnet onto it, with a phone

TAILNET BRIDGES

  A bridge is a phone with two legs: WiFi to the house, USB-C Ethernet to
  something that has no way onto the tailnet of its own.

      house WiFi --wlan0-- [ phone ] --lan0-- the segment
                               |
                        tailscaled advertises 10.99.x.0/24

  Two of them, for two problems that look nothing alike until you write them
  down:

    tailnet-bridge-moose-bmc   moose's BMC has a dedicated management port and
      (Librem 5)               nothing else. Behind this bridge, moose is
                               reachable while it is powered off, hung, or has
                               no working OS -- and the BMC's own credentials
                               revert to factory defaults on a power loss, so
                               the tailnet policy is the only access control
                               worth relying on.

    tailnet-bridge-generic     rpi3 and rpi4 sit on a cable. The rpi3 is not
      (PinePhone)              provisioned and has no DNS entry; neither has a
                               tailnet identity of its own. Behind this bridge
                               a workspace can reach a board without anything
                               on the house network reaching either.

  `wk bridge ls` lists what is declared and what answers.

THE TWO LANES

  A bridge runs postmarketOS, which is what makes one role and one builder
  cover both phones: pmOS carries `pine64-pinephone` and `purism-librem5` in the
  same tree, with the same OpenRC, the same apk and the same NetworkManager. The
  hand-built Librem 5 this replaces ran PureOS and was written in systemd,
  Debian's NetworkManager and apt -- none of which was portable to a second
  phone.

  One command does the whole path:

      wk bridge provision tailnet-bridge-generic

  It builds what it needs, writes a card, and then stops -- because the next
  step is a hand. It tells you exactly what to do with the card, the cable, the
  wall charger, the kill switch and the dock; waits; picks the story back up on
  the other side; applies the role; and stops once more for the one thing it
  cannot do, the tailnet policy. Then it checks the route actually came live,
  which is a different claim from "I pasted it" and the only one that matters.

  WHERE IT INSTALLS: the phone's internal storage, and there is no second
  option. A bridge writes leases, logs and tailscale state for months, and a
  microSD is the part of a phone most likely to wear out or work loose -- so the
  card is the rescue copy, never the system. Writing the bridge image to the card
  and letting the phone boot that is quicker to reach and used to be offered
  here; it is gone, because it left the bridge living on the one component most
  likely to fail. GETTING IT ONTO INTERNAL STORAGE below has the mechanism.

  A phone Jumpdrive does not cover is refused by name rather than quietly
  falling back to the card. Today that means the Librem 5, and the refusal names
  what would fix it: a fetch profile with a pinned URL, its sha256, and
  FET_DEVICE set to that phone's pmOS codename.

  That route is the reason the phone needs a cable: it is plugged into the
  machine holding the card reader, Jumpdrive exports its internal storage as USB
  mass storage, and that storage is then an ordinary removable disk on an
  ordinary machine. Both halves are rpi5 here, and BR_CARD names it once.

  How that disk is identified is worth knowing, because every obvious way is
  wrong. Jumpdrive exports the SD card *as well as* the eMMC, as two separate
  LUNs -- so "the disk that appeared" is normally two, and one of them is the
  Jumpdrive currently running. Writing to that one destroys the tool being used
  to do the writing and leaves the eMMC untouched. And the three things that
  look like discriminators are not: LUN order works until a release reorders it,
  both LUNs are the same gadget so they carry identical model strings, and the
  sizes overlap (16/32/128 GB of eMMC against whatever card you own).

  What is not a guess: we wrote the Jumpdrive card ourselves, minutes earlier,
  and verified the read-back. So provision hashes the first mebibyte of each
  exported disk and compares it with the image it just wrote -- the card is the
  one that matches, and the eMMC is what is left, with nothing recognised and no
  ordering assumed. It is fully automatic and there is no prompt. The length of
  each read is checked as well as its hash, because a disk that reads zero bytes
  hashes to the sha256 of the empty string: a valid-looking digest that matches
  nothing, which unguarded would classify an unreadable disk as the eMMC and aim
  a write at it. Anything that leaves the answer undecided -- no disk matching
  the card, or two -- stops the run and prints what it saw.

  Nothing in that chain holds a tailscale credential, and that is deliberate
  rather than unfinished. Minting a tagged auth key and writing the policy both
  need an API key, and a repository that held a policy-write one would be a
  repository whose compromise rewrites every ACL you have. So provision asks and
  waits at those two points instead of acquiring the ability to do them itself.

  Every step is skipped when it is already done, so the way to use it after
  something goes wrong is to run it again. Two flags for the cases it cannot
  infer:

      --no-write    the phone already holds the right system; go straight to
                    waiting for it and applying the role
      --rebuild     build a fresh image rather than using the newest in the
                    store (--image <id> picks a specific one instead)
      --disk        write the card somewhere other than BR_CARD, for a day the
                    reader is a different machine

  The pieces are still commands, and provision is a chain of them rather than a
  replacement -- which is what makes a half-finished run recoverable by hand.
  The card route:

      wk sysimage build bridge-pinephone            # a pmOS system for the phone
      wk sysimage write <id> --disk rpi5:/dev/mmcblk0
      # ...card into the phone, power on...
      wk bridge setup tailnet-bridge-generic        # the role, over ssh

  ...and the eMMC route is the same list with Jumpdrive in front of it, which is
  spelled out under GETTING IT ONTO INTERNAL STORAGE below.

  `wk bridge setup` on its own is also the *re*-provisioning path, and the one
  to reach for after any change to a conf or to bridge/provision.sh: it is
  idempotent, it needs no card, and it reports how many things it changed.

  The image is built by pmbootstrap on a Linux aarch64 machine over ssh --
  `rpi5` by default -- because pmbootstrap is Linux-only and needs root, and the
  workstation driving this is a Mac. Both phones are aarch64, so the build host
  runs the target's own architecture and nothing is emulated; the builder
  refuses a host that is not aarch64 rather than quietly taking ten times as
  long. Minutes, with a warm package cache.

  What goes into the image, so that the phone is reachable the first time it
  boots and `wk bridge setup` has something to talk to:

    the ssh key      $WK_IMAGE_KEY, or ~/.ssh/id_ed25519.pub
    the WiFi         copied from the build host's own connection, on the build
                     host, so the PSK never travels through a log or an agent's
                     context. This is why the build host has to be a machine on
                     the WiFi the phone will use -- a phone has no cable, and an
                     image without a credential boots into isolation.
    the hostname     the bridge's name
    the packages     the whole bridge role's package list, so provisioning needs
                     no egress and no apk index that has moved on
    the console      user `user`, password `147147` -- the phone's own screen,
                     which is the recovery path when WiFi and ssh are both gone.
                     Password authentication over the network is off.
    mDNS             avahi, with the service enabled in the image. On the very
                     first boot there is no tailnet name yet -- `wk bridge
                     setup` is what creates it -- so `<hostname>.local` is the
                     whole of first contact, and setup falls back to it by
                     itself.

  The image carries the device's own bootloader: pmbootstrap embeds it at the
  offset the device declares (`deviceinfo_sd_embed_firmware`, in units of 1024
  bytes -- 8 KiB on a PinePhone, 33 KiB on a Librem 5). The builder checks it is
  there rather than writing it, because writing it by hand overlapped
  pmbootstrap's copy and turned a working image into an unbootable card.

GETTING IT ONTO INTERNAL STORAGE

  A card is not where a bridge should live: a bridge writes leases, logs and
  tailscale state for months, and a microSD is the part of a phone most likely to
  wear out or work loose. So the verified image goes onto internal storage and
  the card is the rescue copy -- put it back in and the phone boots Jumpdrive
  again, with the install underneath untouched.

  This is what `wk bridge provision` does by default, and it is worth reading the
  long form once, because every step of it is a command this repo already had:

      wk sysimage build recovery-pinephone      # Jumpdrive, pinned and stored
      wk sysimage write <that id> --disk rpi5:/dev/mmcblk0
      # card into the phone, phone cabled to rpi5, power on
      wk sysimage disks rpi5                    # the phone's eMMC is now a disk
      wk sysimage write <bridge image> --disk rpi5:/dev/sdX

  `wk bridge provision <name>` is that list with the waiting done for you, the
  eMMC identified by difference rather than by eye, and the role applied at the
  end. The pieces stay usable on their own: a run that dies halfway leaves a
  card with Jumpdrive on it and a phone that still boots it, which is exactly
  where a re-run picks up.

  Jumpdrive is the PinePhone's service image: it boots from a card and exports
  the phone's internal storage as USB mass storage. That turns "flash the
  phone's eMMC" into "write a removable disk attached to a machine", which is
  `wk sysimage write` with every refusal and the block-map path it already has,
  and no new verb.

  There was a second route -- write the image from the card system to the idle
  eMMC over ssh -- and it is deliberately gone. It reimplemented dd, partition
  growth and identity-copying against a phone, to reach a disk that Jumpdrive
  hands to the ordinary write path for nothing.

  Then: power off (not reboot -- the card wins a boot), take the card out, power
  on, and `wk bridge setup <name>` to re-apply the role to the installed system.
  It is idempotent and every package it needs is already in the image. provision
  prompts for exactly this and then does the last part itself.

  microSD, and both phones boot one in preference to their internal storage.
  Neither depends on what is already installed: the image carries each device's
  own firmware at the offset its deviceinfo names -- `deviceinfo_sd_embed_firmware`,
  in units of 1024 bytes, so the PinePhone's SPL at 8 KiB and the Librem 5's
  u-boot at 33 KiB, which are the canonical sunxi and i.MX addresses. So the card
  carries its own way of starting. That is what
  makes this the recoverable path: the eMMC install underneath is untouched, and
  removing the card puts the phone back exactly as it was.

  The build host needs a few packages of its own (kpartx, bmap-tools,
  python3-venv, python3-yaml). They are not in host/linux/apt.txt because
  multipath-tools brings a multipathd daemon no other Linux host here wants, so
  the builder asks once, on the machine that needs them.

  What it costs on the build host: about 8 GB under ~/wk-pmos for pmbootstrap's
  chroots and package cache -- which is what makes a rebuild four minutes rather
  than twenty -- plus half a gigabyte per kept build under ~/wk-pmos/out.
  `wk disk` reports both, on every build host a profile names; `wk gc` prunes
  interrupted and superseded builds, and `wk gc --purge-pmos` erases the chroots
  when you want the 8 GB back.

  Long builds detach: `--detach` returns as soon as the build is running on the
  build host, and `--resume` picks it back up and imports it. The build survives
  the connection either way -- it is started with setsid on the far side, which
  is the only reason a dropped WiFi link costs a poll rather than half an hour.

WHAT IS PINNED

  pmbootstrap        3.9.0, a git tag. Every PyPI release of it is yanked, so
                     pip installs it from the project's own repository.
  the pmOS channel   v25.12 -- what pmaports' channels.cfg calls the latest
                     release (Alpine 3.23). Not edge: a device whose job is to
                     still be answering in six months does not want a rolling
                     release. A channel has to be one channels.cfg lists; a
                     branch that merely exists (v26.06 does) is not a channel.
  the device names   pine64-pinephone, purism-librem5

  Both live in image/profiles.sh. The pmaports commit that was actually used is
  recorded in the image's manifest, so `wk sysimage show <id>` says what a card
  was built from.

DECLARING AND PROVISIONING ONE

  A bridge is a conf file. Shared, in this repository:

      bridge/hosts/<name>.conf

  ...or this device's own view of one, which wins where both exist:

      ~/.config/wk/bridges/<name>.conf

  Most of the fields are documented at the top of bridge/provision.sh, which is
  the script they are handed to. Three worth thinking about:

    BR_EGRESS    none or nat. Whether the segment may reach the internet. A
                 test board needs it (`wk pi setup` downloads tailscale onto
                 the board itself); a BMC must not have it.
    BR_REACHED_BY  who may reach the segment, in tailnet-policy terms. Printed
                 by setup for you to paste. tag:wk belongs on the board
                 segment and must never appear on the BMC's: root on a bridge
                 is equivalent to whatever is behind it, and a compromised
                 container should not be one hop from a machine's firmware.
    BR_CARD      <machine>:<device> -- the reader this bridge's cards get
                 written on, e.g. rpi5:/dev/mmcblk0. The one field the phone
                 never sees: it is read by `wk bridge provision` on the
                 workstation and is a property of the reader, not of the phone,
                 so `--disk` overrides it for a day the card is somewhere else.
                 Declared rather than discovered because picking the wrong
                 /dev/sd* is the one mistake in this path that destroys
                 something that was not yours to destroy.

  There is no BR_PROFILE, deliberately: image/profiles.sh already records which
  bridge each pmos profile builds (PMO_BRIDGE), and the same fact in two files
  is a fact that drifts. provision derives the profile from that.

  Then:

      wk bridge provision <name>      # the whole path, prompting at the two
                                      # steps that need a hand
      wk bridge setup <name>          # idempotent; re-run after any change
                                      # (WK_BRIDGE_AUTHKEY=tskey-... to skip the
                                      #  prompt on a first run)
      wk bridge status <name>         # the on-device health check, read-only
      wk bridge rm <name>             # removes the role, leaves the OS

  The one thing setup cannot do is apply the tailnet policy: it prints the
  tagOwners, autoApprovers and grant to paste into the admin console. Applying
  it would need an API key with policy-write scope, and this repository holding
  one would mean its compromise rewrites every ACL you have.

  Without `autoApprovers` for the segment the bridge comes up looking perfectly
  healthy and nothing behind it is reachable. `wk bridge status` calls that out
  specifically, because it is the failure that looks like success -- and
  `wk bridge provision` will not report a bridge finished until the phone's own
  `tailscale status` shows the route as primary.

  It is also a once-per-bridge step, not a once-per-flash one. Once tag:bridge
  and the segment are in the policy, reflashing the phone needs no console visit
  at all: provision checks, finds the route already approved, and says so.

THINGS THAT WILL BITE YOU

  A PinePhone has TWO card slots under the back cover, and the microSD is the
  UPPER one. The lower one is the micro-SIM. A card in the SIM slot gives you
  the exact symptom of a card that is not there at all: the phone boots its
  internal storage and never mentions the card. PINE64 lists "the microSD card
  is in the wrong slot" first among the reasons a PinePhone will not boot one,
  which is a good indication of how often it is that. It cost an afternoon here
  once (2026-08-21), after the image, the write and the SPL offset had all been
  verified byte for byte and were all fine.

  Nothing else about SD boot needs configuring, and it is worth knowing that so
  you do not go looking. The A64 boot ROM loads 32 KB from sector 16 of the SD
  card before it looks at the eMMC at all -- no jumper, no DIP switch, no key
  combination -- and postmarketOS's Tow-Boot, which lives in the eMMC's
  dedicated firmware storage, does not override that. A correctly written card
  always wins the boot. So when one does not, the fault is that the ROM never
  read the card: the wrong slot, a card not engaged, or a phone that was woken
  rather than cold-booted. Inserting a card into a running phone does nothing --
  only a cold boot re-runs the ROM.

  The u-boot residue trap that PINE64 documents does *not* apply to a card this
  repo wrote, and knowing why saves re-reading it: the problem there is a card
  *formatted after* being flashed, which leaves a bootloader at 8 KiB (or at
  128 KiB, for the GPT-based Danctnix images) with no OS behind it. Every write
  here lays down a whole image over the front of the card, both offsets
  included, so there is never a stale bootloader in the region the ROM reads.

  Power the phone from a wall charger, never from the machine it exists to
  rescue. Fed from that machine, the out-of-band path dies exactly when it is
  needed. On mains, the battery is a UPS instead of the entire supply.

  Never `poweroff` a bridge. There is no reliable way to power a phone back on
  remotely. `reboot` is fine -- the hardware watchdog recovers hangs, but not a
  clean shutdown.

  Kill switches: WiFi on. The camera switch is the camera stream's access
  control, and it is hardware: switched off there is no capture device, so
  nothing can stream, and no software fault can undo that.

  The downstream interface is renamed by a udev rule, which applies when the
  adapter appears -- an interface that is already up cannot be renamed
  underneath itself. On a first run with the dock already plugged in, re-plug
  it (or reboot) and run setup again; setup says so when it notices.

  If the interface never appears at all, the dock is refusing a USB Data Role
  Swap. Both phones have one port and default to *device* mode. Powering the
  dock before connecting it to the phone changes the negotiation and is the
  thing worth trying; no amount of configuration fixes a dock that will not
  swap.

  An ssh session dying while the phone stays up is usually a WiFi roam, not a
  fault -- two APs on one SSID and the phone moves between them. Keepalives,
  not investigation. dotfiles/ssh/config sets ServerAliveInterval for the
  bridges for this reason.

  Nothing on the phone is upgraded unattended, deliberately. A kernel bump that
  fails to boot needs the device in your hands: the PinePhone does have a UART,
  on the headphone jack behind one of the DIP switches, but it needs a special
  cable and the back off, and the Librem 5's is not something you have wired. So
  `apk upgrade` when you are in front of it -- and the card is the fallback,
  since rewriting it is one `wk sysimage write` away.
